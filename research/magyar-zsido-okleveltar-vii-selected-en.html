Magyar-Zsidó Oklevéltár VII — English translation of printed pages 136, 306 and 747

Budapest, 1963. Edited by Fülöp Grünvald and Sándor Scheiber. Translation checked against the three page images on 16 October 2026. Claude Opus supplied an independent image-based review; the corrections below were checked against enlarged scans. Names and historical place spellings are retained. Square brackets mark editorial explanations or uncertainty. A dash reproduces an empty/dash cell; it is not a newly inferred zero. This is a translation of the published transcription, not of the archival manuscripts.

Printed page 136 · PDF viewer page 138 · 1728 census

Context supplied from printed page 134: document 15, census of the Jews of Máramaros County, submitted at Sziget on 8 January 1728. The first paragraph below continues an entry begun on the preceding page; its householder is not named on page 136.

Continuation from the preceding page

He has one copper vessel suitable for distilling spirits and trades only in spirits. In the present circumstances he himself does not know what profit he obtains from this. He has already lived for six years in the house of the nobles Stephani and Jonae Pap, otherwise called Ber, without possessing land. He pays the landlords 20 Hungarian florins annually. Toward the village’s allotted contribution, [he pays] Rh. fl. 2·40 [2 Rhenish florins and 40 kreuzer].

He has one copper distilling vessel and trades in the sale of spirits. He has ascertained what this currently brings him [the print reads “in compertum habet”; this differs from the explicit statements of ignorance in the neighbouring entries, and a missing negative is possible but not supplied here]. He lives at the house of Timosi Borodi and has taken refuge/lodging there; he pays nothing either to the landowner or to the village of Butfalva [the village name in the prose differs from the Szerfalva heading].

In the village of Gyulafalva

Name as printedEntry in the columns

He has two copper vessels and trades in the sale of wine and spirits. He himself does not know what this brings in annually. He lives in the house of the noble Stephani Rednik, without possessing any land. He has lived there for three years [the phrase may also qualify the payment]; for the house he pays 10 Hungarian florins annually. For the palatinal quota he pays 4 Rhenish florins annually.

Iszak: Engaging in no trade, he drags out his life with great difficulty [the printed “aeque” is apparently a reading or misprint for “aegre”]. Sometimes he distils spirits to support himself. For two years he has lived in the house of Thomae Barod. Since he lacks the necessities, he pays neither a charge for the house nor a contribution for the village.

The heading spells the village Kulinfalva; the prose spells it Kalinfalva. Sidó, retained in the entries, may be a descriptive word for Jewish rather than an inherited surname. Latin names in prose are retained in their printed, inflected forms.

Jacobus: Living in the said village of Kalinfalva and having absolutely nothing, he pays nothing to anyone.

Column key for page 136

The original columns, in order: leaseholders; their wives; male co-tenants; female co-tenants; sons; daughters; male servants; a second servant column printed “Servites” [probably intended for female servants]; horses; young cattle; cows; oxen. All other numerical cells within the Szerfalva, Gyulafalva and Kalinfalva blocks are blank. In particular, no wife is entered for Moska Nifre; a blank is not evidence that he was unmarried or widowed. The top continuation paragraph has no figures on this page.

Separate research commentary — not part of the source

They document early Jewish inhabitants and census categories in the relevant region. They do not identify ancestors of Jozsef Moldovan Katz. Joszip Isak’s appearance in 1728 predates Jozsef’s estimated birth around 1790 by about 62 years. Similar names, including Berke/Burek, are leads requiring records that connect generations.
