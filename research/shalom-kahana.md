# Shalom Kahana: family and descendant research

Research date: 13 October 2026. This updates the existing Kahan family tree, not Geni or WikiTree.

## Identity and findings

The existing tree identifies **Shalom Kahana (1943–1996)**, son of Chaim Arye Leb Kahana and Leah,
with [Geni profile 6000000009318607641](https://www.geni.com/people/Shalom-Kahana/6000000009318607641).
Hillel Kahana identified this person as his father in the research request on 13 October 2026.
That statement is the source for Hillel’s newly added parent-child link; no dates or other relationships
for Hillel were inferred.

The [memorial created by Shalom’s children](https://sites.google.com/view/kahanas) names his father
Chaim Arye Kahana and wife Ruth. It places his birth in Timișoara in Hebrew year 5703, describes chemistry
studies at Bar-Ilan, teaching at Nehalim, and his eventual principalship at HaRo’eh High School in Ramat Gan.
The civil year 1943 is retained from the existing tree; Hebrew year 5703 alone does not establish it exactly.
The memorial’s historical jurisdiction wording is not adopted as an independently verified fact.
Its [ethical-will transcription](https://sites.google.com/view/kahanas/צוואתו) is dated Av 5755 (1995);
it addresses his wife and children without naming each child. The transcription was read, not an original scan.

The existing arrival citation remains: Israel State Archives, immigrant lists January–February 1951,
GL-15496/1, page 167, rows 480–482, Transylvania, 1 February 1951.
[Existing image reference](https://journaliststudio.google.com/pinpoint/document-view?collection=585158a3cc8e02af&p=1&docid=ec7469b5b0ffa001_585158a3cc8e02af&dapvm=2).
This record was not re-inspected during the present pass.

## Descendants added

[Rivki Goldfinger’s interview with Michal Tikochinsky](https://www.inn.co.il/news/406366), published
4 July 2019 and updated 7 July, identifies her parents as Shalom and Ruth, her husband as Rabbi Yakir,
and their seven children: **Eliav, Renana, Avital, Yitzhak, Naomi, Shulamit, and Tamar**.
These seven relationships are explicit family testimony in a published interview. Only the given names
are entered; surnames, exact birth dates and subsequent families are not inferred.
Hillel is added separately as Shalom’s child on the user's direct testimony. Ruth and Yakir are added as spouses.
Michal and Rachel were already present. Rachel’s existing 1972–1972 entry remains based on the earlier
[Geni citation](https://www.geni.com/people/Rachel-Kahana/6000000009327679384), not newly verified evidence.

## Earlier coverage and conflicts (superseded where noted below)

- **The complete descendant list is not established.** The interview describes Michal as the second of 13 children.
  The saved Geni search result instead presents four children plus fourteen others. The old tree converted this
  to 16 unnamed children beside two named children. That exact placeholder count has been removed from the display
  because it conflicts with direct family testimony; the underlying discrepancy is preserved here.
- Whether the interview’s count includes Rachel, who died in infancy, is not established. No arithmetic has been
  used to create unnamed people. Geni duplicates, step-relationships or other explanations remain untested.
- Michal’s birthplace differs: the interview gives Ramat Gan and a move to Bnei Brak at age three;
  [Hebrew Wikipedia, revision 43100779](https://he.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=מיכל_טיקוצ%27ינסקי&oldid=43100779)
  gives Bnei Brak. The discrepancy is noted rather than silently resolved.
- Shalom’s memorial gives chemistry studies; the interview describes biochemistry. The tree uses the family
  memorial’s wording, without claiming a verified degree title.
- The remaining children of Shalom, their spouses, grandchildren and later generations require a named family
  roster or an accessible source linking each generation. Michal’s children’s later families are also unresolved.

## Dov Ber parentage evidence audit

The question is whether **Dov Ber Bernat Kahan** is demonstrably the son of the specific **Chaim Arye Leb
Moldovan Kahan (1827–1897)** shown in the tree. The current evidence supports the connection but does not justify
an unqualified claim of certainty.

1. [WikiTree Kahan-376](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Kahan-376), biography read through its public API
   on 14 October 2026, cites [JOWBR J_MARAMURE_0008659](https://www.jewishgen.org/databases/cemetery/jowbr.php?rec=J_MARAMURE_0008659)
   as Dov, son of Chaim Aryeh HaKohen; Rosh Chodesh Shevat 5688; age 56; Giulesti. This is an explicit
   patronymic in a cited index transcription. The original inscription was not inspected in this audit.
   The patronymic alone does not uniquely identify the father with the Moldovan Kahan profile.
2. [WikiTree Moldovan_Kahan-1](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Moldovan_Kahan-1) cites Majer Jakab’s death,
   retrospectively registered as **1902-229**, Romanian National Archives, Baia Mare, Inventory 1373;
   the saved project transcription identifies **Book 41**. It names parents **Leb Kahan and Freide Klein**
   and says **Bernat Kahan registered the death**. It does not explicitly identify the informant as a brother.
3. Brana’s death, **1898-10**, Inventory 1371, Book 53 in the saved project transcription, names her father
   Bernat and describes **Izrael Junger as the father’s brother-in-law**. Leb’s death, **1897-76**, names
   **Sztrul Junger as the deceased’s son-in-law**. These offer a collateral-family bridge, but the identity
   of Izrael/Sztrul and the precise marriage link need verification rather than being assumed from the names.

The existing parent link is retained with an explicit evidence note. A decisive next record would be an original
birth, marriage or death entry for Dov/Bernat naming his parents, or the original gravestone combined with records
that distinguish this Chaim Aryeh from namesakes. WikiTree’s linked parent field is not independent evidence.
The birth-year discrepancy (about 1868 versus about 1871/72) also remains relevant to identity checks.
Live web-reader attempts to open WikiTree and JOWBR failed; WikiTree’s biography API succeeded. The failure to
retrieve the burial image is an access limitation, not proof that an image does not exist.
